# 00 — Dental Market Analysis

## Why dentists are a good vertical for FABLE

1. **They already spend on marketing.** Dental practices routinely budget
   $2,000–$10,000+/month on patient acquisition. A website + automation
   package is a rounding error against one new high-value patient
   (an implant case can be worth $3,000–$6,000; ortho $5,000+).
2. **Their pain maps exactly onto what FABLE already sells.** Missed
   after-hours calls, no online booking, no recall follow-up, weak review
   presence — these are the same "lead capture / scheduling friction /
   manual operations" pain points the Business Pain Loop already detects.
3. **The buying unit is small.** Owner-dentist or office manager decides.
   One or two conversations can close; the CEO Field Mode capture flow fits
   the sales motion as-is.
4. **It's repeatable.** Practices differ far less than general small
   businesses do. One good template + one automation stack serves the
   majority — which is exactly what the directive's success criteria demand.

## What a dental practice actually buys

| Need | What they say | What we sell |
|------|---------------|--------------|
| New patients | "We want more implant/Invisalign cases" | Website with service pages built to convert + local SEO |
| Fewer missed calls | "Front desk can't keep up" | After-hours lead capture, web booking request form |
| Full chairs | "Too many no-shows and open slots" | Appointment reminder + recall/reactivation automation |
| Reputation | "Competitor has 400 Google reviews" | Post-visit review-request automation |
| Insurance clarity | "Patients always ask if we take X" | Insurance/financing content pattern on the site |

## Buying triggers (when they're ready to move)

- New practice opening or ownership transition (buying a retiring dentist's practice)
- New associate added — need to fill a second column of the schedule
- Just invested in high-margin service line (implants, clear aligners) that needs cases
- Embarrassed by their current site ("built by my nephew in 2014")

## Competitive landscape

The incumbent competitors are dental-specific marketing agencies
(ProSites, PBHS, Great Dental Websites) selling $300–$700/month template
sites, and local generalist agencies. FABLE's edge is **speed plus
automation**: incumbents sell brochures; we sell a lead-capture and
follow-up machine, delivered in weeks, on a modern stack, with the loop
system giving the client visible progress.

## Constraints and cautions (see 01 for platform implications)

- **HIPAA adjacency.** A marketing site itself is not a covered system, but
  the moment forms collect health information ("what procedure are you
  interested in?", "describe your pain") or automation touches appointment
  data, we are handling PHI-adjacent data. Forms must be minimal by design;
  anything integrating with practice-management systems (Dentrix, Eaglesoft,
  Open Dental) needs a BAA conversation. This is a *sales objection we must
  answer confidently*, so the platform needs a standard posture.
- **Regulated advertising.** Some states restrict dental advertising claims
  (before/after photos, "painless", specialty claims). Template copy must
  stay conservative by default.
- **We have one dental data point.** Riverside Family Dental (delivered,
  Growth + Automation, $4–8k) is our only prior. That's a proof point for
  the proposal, not a dataset. Confidence ratings in dental recommendations
  should start Medium until ~5 dental clients are in the system.

## Revenue model for the vertical

- **Build:** Dental Practice Package at $6,000–$12,000 (between Growth and
  Pro tiers) — site + booking capture + automation setup, 4–5 weeks.
- **Recur:** $300–$600/month care plan (hosting, content updates, automation
  monitoring, monthly report). Recurring revenue is the real prize; the
  incumbents prove dentists will pay monthly indefinitely.
- **Target:** first 3 dental clients in 90 days via the CEO's network +
  Riverside Family Dental referrals (dentists know dentists — study clubs,
  suppliers, local dental societies).
